# Compute holidays, borrow school feeds, skip bins

For all four countries, the best free route to public holidays is the one the app already uses for Germany: work them out on the phone from rules written in law. Portugal's 308 municipal holidays and Spain's regional holidays are the exceptions. Those should come from **OpenHolidays**, which is free, needs no key, carries the ODbL licence (commercial use allowed with attribution) and is run by the same small German company that likely already supplies your Ferien data. School holidays split cleanly. **Portugal and Spain can have a shared feed per region**, built from OpenHolidays plus a small table you keep by hand, because OpenHolidays had **no Spanish 2026/27 school year loaded on 18 September 2026**, after classes had already started. **Brazil and the US have no free school-holiday source to share at all**. Calendars there are set per network or per district (the US has more than 13,000 districts), so the existing "paste an ICS link" tile is the answer. Waste collection is the weakest of the three. Most Portuguese, Spanish and Brazilian city households use shared street containers that are emptied daily, so a "bins tomorrow" reminder means little to them. No vendor there covers many towns at once, the way the 13 German families do. The US does have such platforms (ReCollect, Recycle Coach, WM, Republic), but none of them grants a reuse licence. The ranked plan below therefore ships holidays in all four countries first, school holidays in PT and ES second, and treats waste as "paste your own link, or type in your own weekly rule" everywhere, with exactly two open-data cities (Lisbon and Belo Horizonte) worth an adapter later.

## Holidays are mostly computable, and the exceptions are tables, not feeds

The German design works because German law fixes the *rule*: "Easter + 39 days" stays true forever. Portugal, Brazil and the US mostly work the same way.

**Portugal.** The **13 mandatory national holidays** are listed in Código do Trabalho art. 234. They are fixed dates plus Good Friday, Easter and Corpus Christi (Easter + 60) ([Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/legislacao-consolidada/lei/2009-34546475-73982045)). Carnaval (Easter − 47) is optional but closes schools. Madeira adds 2 April, 1 July and 26 December, and the Azores add Whit Monday (Easter + 50) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Portugal)). The hard tier is the **one municipal holiday in each of 308 concelhos**, set by each câmara ([Santander](https://www.santander.pt/salto/feriados-municipais-portugal-2026)). No government dataset of them exists: dados.gov.pt has none, and the DGAEP page returned 404 ([dados.gov.pt](https://dados.gov.pt/pt/datasets/?q=feriados)).

OpenHolidays fills that gap. Queried live, it returned **156 Portuguese entries for 2026, with municipal holidays tagged by municipality code** (for example Lisboa on 13 June, Porto on 24 June), and 2027 is already loaded ([OpenHolidays PT](https://openholidaysapi.org/PublicHolidays?countryIsoCode=PT&validFrom=2026-01-01&validTo=2026-12-31)). Its own README still claims only "national and district" coverage, so the README is out of date, not the data ([data repo](https://github.com/openpotato/openholidaysapi.data)). Portugal therefore needs a rules file for the national and regional days, plus a 308-row table seeded from OpenHolidays' ODbL data. The table should store each rule, meaning a fixed day or an Easter offset, rather than the dates, so it stays correct year after year.

**Brazil** has the same shape at a larger scale. National holidays are fixed in federal law, and 20 November has been national since Lei 14.759/2023 ([Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2023/lei/l14759.htm)). Each state has **exactly one "data magna"** under Lei 9.093/1995, though some states add others. Each municipality may declare up to four religious holidays, and Good Friday counts as one of those four ([Guia Trabalhista mirror](https://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/l9093.htm)). Two traps matter for the display. **Carnaval and Corpus Christi are "pontos facultativos", not holidays.** The 2026 federal portaria lists 10 holidays and 9 pontos facultativos ([SINAIT](https://www.sinait.org.br/noticia/23028/portaria-do-mgi-divulga-feriados-nacionais-e-estabelece-os-dias-de-ponto-facultativo-em-2026)). BrasilAPI labels all of them "national", Easter Sunday included, so it is only good as a cross-check ([BrasilAPI 2026](https://brasilapi.com.br/api/feriados/v1/2026)). For the 5,570 municipalities, the only free bulk source is **joaopbini/feriados-brasil**. It is MIT-licensed, keyed on the IBGE municipality code, and ships CSV/JSON per year through 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/joaopbini/feriados-brasil)). It is a community compilation with no visible update date, so treat it as "probably right".

**The United States** is the easiest case. **5 U.S.C. 6103 fixes 11 federal holidays** as dates or nth-weekday rules, with a Saturday holiday observed on Friday and a Sunday holiday on Monday ([LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/6103); [OPM](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/federal-holidays/)). OPM publishes an ICS file, but a plain server request got an Akamai "Access Denied" page, which is one more reason to compute rather than fetch. State holidays (Good Friday, Lincoln's Birthday, Truman Day, Columbus vs. Indigenous Peoples' Day) are best **ported by hand from python-holidays**. It is MIT-licensed, covers 50 states, DC and the territories, and was updated two days before this research ([united_states.py](https://github.com/vacanza/holidays/blob/dev/holidays/countries/united_states.py)). Nuance for the UI: a US state holiday closes state offices, not schools or most employers. Show federal holidays by default and state holidays as an optional layer.

**Spain is the one country where the German model breaks.** The rule is not fixed. Only a list is, and only for one year. Every October a BOE resolution names each comunidad's choices and its Sunday-to-Monday moves. The 2026 list is **BOE-A-2025-21667, dated 17 October 2025**, and even that has a sub-regional exception: the Val d'Aran swaps 26 December for 17 June ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2025-21667)). The 2027 resolution should therefore arrive around October 2026. Every library copies this document by hand, python-holidays included ([spain.py](https://github.com/vacanza/holidays/blob/dev/holidays/countries/spain.py)). Checked against the BOE, **OpenHolidays' 2026 regional list had at least three errors**:
- a "Martes de Carnaval" for Extremadura on a Friday that the BOE does not list;
- a second Corpus Christi for Castilla-La Mancha;
- 2 November given to 5 comunidades instead of 9.

Sources: [BOE XML](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/xml.php?id=BOE-A-2025-21667) against [OpenHolidays ES](https://openholidaysapi.org/PublicHolidays?countryIsoCode=ES&languageIsoCode=ES&validFrom=2026-01-01&validTo=2026-12-31).

The practical design for Spain is a **yearly hand-checked table of about 20 rows per comunidad, updated each October from the BOE**.

**Spain's local holidays** are the next problem: two per municipality, across about 8,000 municipalities. Official open data covers roughly half the country:
- **Catalonia**: Socrata JSON keyed by INE code ([Transparència Catalunya](https://analisi.transparenciacatalunya.cat/Treball/Calendari-de-festes-locals-a-Catalunya/b4eh-r8up)).
- **Madrid**: a Latin-1 CSV ([datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/es/catalogo/a13002908-festivos-regionales-y-locales-ano-en-curso1)).
- **Euskadi**: a REST API with 2010–2026 data ([Open Data Euskadi](https://opendata.euskadi.eus/catalogo/-/calendario-laboral-de-euskadi-para-el-2026/)).
- **Andalucía**: an API with 1,548 local rows for 2026 and 2027 already present, but keyed by municipality *name* ([datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/es/catalogo/a01002820-calendario-de-dias-festivos-en-la-comunidad-autonoma-de-andalucia)).
- **Galicia**: CC BY-SA 4.0, with 2027 already published ([datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/en/catalogo/a12002994-calendario-laboral-2026)).
- **Also available**: Castilla y León and Baleares (Baleares uses free-text Catalan dates).
- **Missing or stale**: Extremadura (2025 only), Castilla-La Mancha, La Rioja, Valencia, Murcia, Canarias, Cantabria, Ceuta and Melilla.

This is the Abfall vendor-registry pattern again, one adapter per comunidad, but the formats differ enough that every adapter is its own small project. The community repo **ChemieuroDEV/festivos-locales** claims all 8,025 Spanish municipalities and all 308 Portuguese concelhos. It gives no clear licence and names its source as an unexplained "Chemieuro API", so use it to check your data, not as something the app depends on ([GitHub](https://github.com/ChemieuroDEV/festivos-locales)).

## Aggregators: only OpenHolidays and python-holidays belong in the build

The table below ranks every cross-country source the notes examined by fitness for a commercial, privacy-first app.

| Source | PT | ES | BR | US | Licence / cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenHolidays** | National + 308 municipal holidays; national school holidays | Regional holidays (with errors); school holidays by comunidad/province, but **2026/27 missing** | National only, nothing after 2025 | Not covered | ODbL data, free, no key | **Use for PT and ES**; copy the data into your own database rather than calling it live |
| **python-holidays** | Districts + Azores/Madeira | All comunidades, from the BOE | 27 states + São Paulo city | 50 states + DC + territories | MIT | **Use as the reference for hand-porting BR/US rules to Dart** |
| **date-holidays (npm)** | National only | Down to Canary islands | States + 6 capitals | States + some cities | Code ISC, **data CC BY-SA 3.0** (from Wikipedia) | Skip; share-alike is a worse fit than MIT/ODbL |
| **Nager.Date** | National, no municipal | Regional | 1 state holiday | Observed dates, state tags | **Commercial use requires "active sponsorship"**; self-hosting needs a paid key | Skip; had a wrong Azores date |
| **Calendarific** | – | – | – | – | Free tier 500 calls/month with attribution; $100–4,000/yr | Skip; paid, keyed, data updated quarterly |
| **Google holiday ICS** | – | – | 275 events | Yes | No stated licence; undocumented endpoint | Skip as a feature; users can paste it themselves |
| **workalendar** | – | – | – | – | MIT, last pushed April 2024 | Skip; stale |
| **Home Assistant waste_collection_schedule** | Lisbon only | None | None | ~73 sources, about half via ReCollect/Recycle Coach | MIT code; vendor endpoints unlicensed | Reference only |

Sources: [OpenHolidays countries](https://openholidaysapi.org/Countries); [python-holidays](https://github.com/vacanza/holidays); [date-holidays LICENSE](https://github.com/commenthol/date-holidays/blob/master/LICENSE); [Nager ToS](https://nagerholidays.com/legal/termsofservice); [Calendarific pricing](https://calendarific.com/pricing); [HACS README](https://github.com/mampfes/hacs_waste_collection_schedule).

**Why OpenHolidays is first.** It is the only aggregator with Portuguese municipal holidays and with school holidays for either Iberian country. It is ODbL, it needs no key, and it answered without rate-limit headers ([OpenHolidays](https://www.openholidaysapi.org/en/)). ODbL allows commercial use. Showing the dates in the app needs an attribution line. Share-alike applies only if you publish a derived *database*. That reading is an inference from the licence text and has not been confirmed by a lawyer.

**Its two weaknesses.** First, it is a small project run by STÜBER SYSTEMS GmbH, with an unknown funding model. Second, its data can lag or be wrong, as the Spanish school gap and the BOE mismatches show. Two habits cover both. Copy its data into your own table (the existing `public_feeds` row pattern, or vendored files from its GitHub repo), so the phone never contacts it and an outage cannot blank the calendar. And send corrections upstream as pull requests, which also builds goodwill with the maintainer.

**Nager and date-holidays.** Nager.Date is the aggregator most people find first. Its code is MIT, but its **terms require sponsorship for commercial use and forbid running a holiday portal from the data**, and a spot check found the Azores holiday a week late ([Nager README](https://github.com/nager/Nager.Date)). date-holidays is technically attractive because it runs in Deno, the runtime Supabase Edge Functions use. Its package.json says CC-BY-3.0 while its LICENSE file says CC BY-SA 3.0, so treat it as the stricter one.

**Dart packages.** No Dart port of either library exists. `feriados_pt` and `world_holidays` are on pub.dev, but their licences and data sources are **unverified**, so hand-porting the rules in the style of `german_holidays.dart` remains the safe path.

## School holidays work as a shared feed only in Portugal and Spain

**Portugal: national and multi-year.** Mainland public schools follow **Despacho n.º 8368/2024, which fixes four school years at once (2024/25–2027/28)** ([DR](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/despacho/8368-2024-873447631)). It was amended for 2025/26 by Despacho 9989/2025, so amendments mid-cycle do happen ([DR](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/despacho/9989-2025-932720956)). For 2026/27 the breaks are Christmas 16–31 December, Carnaval 8–10 February and Easter 22 March–2 April. The year ends on 4, 11 or 30 June depending on the grade ([CGD](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/formacao-e-tecnologia/Pages/calendario-escolar.aspx)). These dates come from secondary sites and should be checked against the Despacho PDF before you enter them.

The islands differ. **Madeira** issues a binding yearly despacho ([calendarioescolar.pt](https://calendarioescolar.pt/calendario-escolar-madeira/)). **The Azores** publish reference dates only, and each school sets its own. Portaria 909/2026 moved some start dates just weeks before term ([Portal da Educação Açores](https://edu.azores.gov.pt/seccoes/calendario-escolar-2025-2026-a-2027-2028/)). OpenHolidays lists only 7 nationwide Portuguese school entries through 2027, with no island split.

The right product for Portugal is therefore **a hand-kept table of about four ranges a year for three regions (Continente, Madeira, Açores)**. Label the Azores dates "datas de referência", and show Carnaval and the local municipal holiday as school closures too. Because the mainland Despacho binds only public schools, private schools fall back to paste-a-link.

**Spain: regional, with some official feeds.** Each comunidad sets its own school calendar, and Andalucía and Aragón split theirs further by province. OpenHolidays covered all 19 regions for 2025/26, **167 entries, then returned zero for 2026/27** ([OpenHolidays](https://www.openholidaysapi.org/en/)). Only a few regions publish official machine-readable data:
- **Galicia** publishes an ICS/CSV already out for 2026/27. The download URL changes each year, so it is not a stable feed ([Portal Abertos](https://abertos.xunta.gal/es/catalogo/ensino-formacion/-/dataset/0698/calendario-escolar-2026-2027)).
- **Andalucía** exports ICS per school through its Secretaría Virtual, under "consult the Consejería" terms ([Junta de Andalucía](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/datosabiertos/portal/dataset/calendario-escolar-del-curso-actual)).
- **Catalonia** publishes three years ahead (2026–2029), but as a PDF ([Jovecat](https://jovecat.gencat.cat/ca/actualitat/noticies/detalls/Noticia/Calendari-escolar-dels-cursos-2026-2027-2027-2028-i-2028-2029-centres-no-universitaris)).
- **Everything else** is an order in a regional bulletin.

A reliable Spanish Ferien feature means **one row per comunidad (per province in Andalucía and Aragón), filled from OpenHolidays when it is current and by hand each spring when it is not**. That is roughly 6–10 date ranges per region per year. Days each municipality or school sets off for itself will be missing, so call the feature "regional school holidays".

**Brazil: no free source.** No state or municipal network publishes an ICS, JSON or CSV. São Paulo, Rio Grande do Sul, Paraná, Goiás, Rio and the DF all publish PDFs or HTML pages ([RS PDF](https://educacao.rs.gov.br/upload/arquivos/202601/07115929-calendario-escolar-2026-da-rede-estadual.pdf); [SEDUC-SP](https://atendimento.educacao.sp.gov.br/knowledgebase/article/SED-08404/pt-br)). A single city runs a state network, a municipal network and private schools on separate calendars. There is no region to share a feed across without also asking which network the child attends.

**United States: no free source, and 13,000+ calendars.** Each of the **more than 13,000 districts** sets its own calendar ([NCES EDGE](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/edge/Geographic/DistrictBoundaries)). The aggregators that sell school-calendar APIs are paid or have unknown terms ([schools-calendar.com](https://schools-calendar.com/)). The good news is that the large district website platforms, including Finalsite, SchoolMessenger Presence, Edlio and Apptegy, give each calendar an iCal subscribe URL ([Finalsite Support](https://www.finalsitesupport.com/hc/en-us/articles/27762554995981-Subscribe-to-a-calendar); [Torrance USD](https://www.tusd.org/calendar/subscribe)). So the existing `ical` tile already solves US school holidays. It needs clear instructions ("district website → Calendar → Subscribe → copy iCal link"), ideally pointing families at the academic calendar rather than the athletics feed. The share of districts that offer ICS rather than only a PDF is unknown.

## Bin reminders mostly don't translate, and US platforms carry legal risk

**Portugal.** Most households carry waste to street containers and ecopontos. APA data puts door-to-door (porta-a-porta) collection at about **2% of selective collection**. That figure is by type or tonnage rather than households served, and its breakdown did not add up cleanly in the source ([APA RARU 2024](https://apambiente.pt/sites/default/files/_Residuos/Producao_Gest%C3%A3o_Residuos/Dados%20RU/2024/raru_2024.pdf)). Where door-to-door collection exists, as in Porto, ERSUC's Aveiro/Coimbra, Valongo and Maia, the schedule is a weekly pattern in a PDF or on a paper calendar handed out on enrolment ([Porto Ambiente](https://www.portoambiente.pt/residuos-urbanos/recolha-porta-a-porta-residencial); [ERSUC](https://www.ersuc.pt/pt/area-de-utilizador/reciclagem-a-porta/)). The one real dataset is **Lisbon's "Circuitos de Recolha", CC0, with API, GeoJSON and CSV**. It was last updated in December 2023 ([dados.gov.pt](https://dados.gov.pt/en/datasets/circuitos-de-recolha-de-residuos-urbanos/)).

**Spain.** The story is the same. Large cities empty street containers almost daily. Schedules matter in the **368 Catalan porta-a-porta municipalities** ([portaaporta.cat](https://portaaporta.cat/es/)), in Gipuzkoa's "atez ate" towns, and for bulky-item days. **Madrid publishes its bulky-item zones and annual calendar as geodata** ([Geoportal Madrid](https://geoportal.madrid.es/IDEAM_WBGEOPORTAL/dataset.iam?id=e7207a09-7ead-11ec-b24a-60634c31c0aa)). No Portuguese or Spanish vendor covers many towns the way the 13 German vendor families do, and the Home Assistant project has one Portuguese source and no Spanish one.

**Brazil.** Collection is a **recurring weekday-and-shift pattern per street**, for example "Mon/Wed/Fri, night". It is not a dated list. The capitals offer address or CEP lookups with no documented API: São Paulo, Curitiba, Porto Alegre and Rio's Comlurb ([coleta.prefeitura.sp.gov.br](https://coleta.prefeitura.sp.gov.br/)). **Only Belo Horizonte publishes open data, under CC-BY, updated June 2026** ([dados.pbh.gov.br](https://dados.pbh.gov.br/dataset/dados-georreferenciados-coleta-residuos)), and nobody has yet confirmed that the CSV has day and shift columns. São Paulo is the biggest prize. The clean route there is a written request to SP Regula or an access-to-information (LAI) request, not reverse-engineering its lookup.

**United States.** This is the only one of the four countries with German-style platforms. **ReCollect**, owned by Routeware since 2021, serves a per-address ICS feed used by Houston, Austin, Denver, Recology SF, Raleigh and others. Recycle Coach, WM and Republic Services have address lookups ([HACS ReCollect](https://github.com/mampfes/hacs_waste_collection_schedule/blob/master/doc/ics/recollect.md); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/03/25/2199529/0/en/Routeware-Global-Acquires-ReCollect-to-Add-Digital-Engagement-Expertise.html)). None of them licenses reuse:
- ReCollect's terms are **silent** on automated access, which is not the same as permission ([ReCollect ToS](https://api.recollect.net/terms.en-US.html)).
- Home Assistant's own ReCollect integration warns that it uses "not an official API … at your own risk" ([Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/recollect_waste/)).
- WM's keys are embedded in its web app and rotate ([HACS WM](https://github.com/mampfes/hacs_waste_collection_schedule/blob/master/doc/source/wm_com.md)).

City open data such as NYC's gives weekday patterns per zone, not dated pickups. It has not been updated since April 2024 and has no licence field ([NYC Open Data](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/Garbage-Collection-Schedule/p7k6-2pm8)). For a commercial app, the low-risk move is to let the household paste its own ReCollect "Add to Google Calendar" link into the `ical` tile. The household is then fetching its own schedule. Writing server adapters would put Aporah in the position of reselling Routeware's data without a contract.

The cross-country conclusion is that **the missing feature is a household-typed weekly rule** ("restos Mon/Wed/Fri, embalagens Thursday, vidro first Tuesday"). It covers Portuguese and Spanish door-to-door towns and every Brazilian city, and it sends no address anywhere. Aporah already computes due days from a rule for Trackers, so the date logic exists. What it lacks is a place in Kalender or Abfall to enter such a rule and turn it into bin reminders.

## What to build, in order, and what onboarding must ask

The ranking weighs coverage gained against upkeep, licence risk and privacy. "Build" means inside the app or as one shared server row per region, never a per-household call to a third party.

| Rank | Portugal | Spain | Brazil | United States |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 – build first** | National + Madeira/Azores holidays computed on device; 308 municipal holidays as a rules table seeded from OpenHolidays (ODbL) | National + comunidad holidays as a yearly table checked against the October BOE (2027 due ~Oct 2026) | National holidays computed; Carnaval/Corpus Christi shown as "ponto facultativo"; 27 state holidays hand-kept | 11 federal holidays computed with observed-day shifts |
| **2** | School holidays: hand table for Continente / Madeira / Açores | School holidays per comunidad (per province in AN/AR): OpenHolidays + hand fill each spring, Galicia ICS | Municipal holidays imported from joaopbini (MIT, IBGE-keyed), refreshed yearly | State holidays ported from python-holidays (MIT) as an optional layer |
| **3** | Typed weekly bin rule | Local holidays via per-comunidad adapters (Catalonia, Madrid, Euskadi, Galicia, Andalucía first) | Typed weekly bin rule | School calendar via pasted ICS with district-specific instructions |
| **4 – later** | Lisbon waste adapter (CC0) | Typed weekly bin rule; Madrid bulky-item zones | Belo Horizonte waste adapter (CC-BY), after checking its columns | Waste via pasted ReCollect/Recycle By City ICS |
| **Skip** | Scraping municipal PDFs; any per-address waste lookup | Chemieuro/festivos-locales as a dependency; urban container schedules | School-holiday feed (no source); scraping SP's CEP lookup | Server adapters for ReCollect/WM/Republic without a contract; paid school-calendar APIs; Nager/Calendarific |

The onboarding step needs **one stable code per country**, with everything else derived from it:

**Portugal.** Capture the **concelho** (308 values, with a picker grouped by region). It drives every holiday and school rule, and the region follows from it, so never ask for the region. Distrito is not a legal tier for any of these features. A street address or CP4-CP3 postcode matters only for Lisbon waste. GEO API PT can turn a postcode into concelho and freguesia, but its data licence is not stated, and Portuguese postcode data is commercially licensed by CTT, so it is **unverified for commercial use** ([geoapi.pt](https://geoapi.pt/docs/)).

**Spain.** Capture the **5-digit INE municipality code**. Province, comunidad and (for Canarias) island follow from it, and it is the join key most datasets use. Use the postcode only to narrow the list, because one postcode can cover several municipalities, and no free postcode-to-INE mapping with a verified licence was found. Allow an optional locality below the municipality for pedanías that have their own fiesta.

**Brazil.** Capture the **CEP**, and resolve it to UF + IBGE code with ViaCEP. ViaCEP is free and keyless, forbids bulk use and redistribution, and suits one lookup per household ([viacep.com.br](https://viacep.com.br/)). Store UF and `codigo_ibge`. If school holidays are ever added, they will also need a school-network choice (estadual, municipal or particular).

**United States.** Capture **street, city, state and 5-digit ZIP**. The state alone drives holidays, and state + ZIP are the minimum for any future waste or district feature. The Census Geocoder returns county and school-district IDs in one keyless call, requires an attribution line, and is public-domain data ([Census Geocoder](https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/Geocoding_Services_API.html); [live test](https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder)). Calling it sends a home address to a US federal server, which is a third-country transfer for EU-hosted Supabase and belongs in the privacy policy. **For a holidays-only launch, a state picker plus ZIP is enough**, and the street address can wait until a feature needs it.

## Licences still unverified before launch

Several terms remain open, and each blocks a specific item above:
- whether ODbL attribution in Settings is enough (legal review recommended);
- each Spanish regional portal's licence (datos.gob.es returned no licence field, and only Galicia's CC BY-SA 4.0 was confirmed);
- BOE reuse terms;
- the licences of `feriados_pt`, `world_holidays`, GEO API PT's data and festivos-locales;
- Nager's sponsorship price;
- ReCollect, WM and Republic's stance on automated access;
- the Census Geocoder's rate limits.

The accuracy of joaopbini/feriados-brasil beyond its README is unchecked. The Portuguese 2026/27 school dates were taken from secondary sites.

## Conclusion

The four markets reward the design Aporah already chose: compute from law, keep one shared row per region, and let the household paste what only it can reach. The real cost of expanding is not API work but **editorial upkeep on a calendar**: the BOE in October, the Spanish school orders in spring, Madeira's despacho in May, and a yearly refresh of Brazilian municipal holidays. That is a few hours a year that a non-technical owner can schedule, unlike a scraper that breaks silently.

The second lesson is that **the German Abfall feature is a German artefact**. Its value came from a vendor ecosystem and door-to-door bins that Iberia and Brazil mostly lack, and where the US has the ecosystem, it lacks the licence. A household-typed weekly bin rule serves more families in these four countries than any adapter would, and it is also the most private option on the list.
